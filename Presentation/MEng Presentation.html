Background

Motivation

  • FSC Lab uses Isaac Sim + Pegasus to develop algorithms for the F450 quadrotor
  • Before sim-to-real transfer: how faithful is the simulation?
  • Tracking error confounds three sources simultaneously
Controller imperfection  +  vehicle-model imperfection  +  simulator imperfection = observed error
Two research questions:
(i)
How faithful is Isaac Sim's PhysX rigid-body simulation to the physics encountered on hardware?
(ii)
How much of any discrepancy is attributable to the simulator versus the vehicle model?
Experimental Design

Controller as a Fixed Measurement Instrument

  • Use the geometric tracking controller (Lee et al., 2010) as a fixed, theoretically characterised instrument
  • Almost-globally exponentially stable on SE(3) — closed-loop behaviour fully known
  • Fix the controller. Vary only the simulation configuration.
  • Any change in tracking error → attributable to modelling assumptions alone
Body-frame convention — Lee et al. (2010)
Vehicle Modelling

F450 CAD Model

  • Full assembly in OnShape: frame, landing gear, Pixhawk, LiPo, 4× motor + prop, wiring box
  • All components modelled to measured dimensions
  • Mass properties (CoG, inertia) extracted from OnShape's mass-properties tool
  • Assembly origin aligned with CoG via mate connector
  • Total CAD mass: 1.806 kg — matches measured vehicle

Geometric Control

Dynamics & Error Coordinates on SE(3)

Quadrotor Dynamics
\(m\ddot{x} = f R e_3 - mg e_3\)

\(J\dot{\Omega} = -\Omega \times J\Omega + M\)
Configuration: \((x,R)\in\mathrm{SE}(3)\), with \(R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)\) and \(\Omega\) expressed in the body frame.
Error Coordinates
\(\Psi(R,R_d)=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(I-R_d^TR)=1-\cos\theta \in [0, 2]\)

\(e_x = x-x_d,\quad e_v = \dot{x}-\dot{x}_d\)

\(e_R=\tfrac{1}{2}(R_d^TR-R^TR_d)^\vee\)

\(e_\Omega=\Omega-R^TR_d\Omega_d\)
\(e_R\in\mathfrak{so}(3)\): no Euler-angle singularities or quaternion sign ambiguity.
Controller Construction
Trajectory tracking
\(A=-k_xe_x-k_ve_v-mge_3+m\ddot{x}_d\)
\(\vec b_{3d}=-A/\|A\|,\quad f=-A\cdot Re_3\)
Attitude tracking
\(\vec b_{2d}=\dfrac{\vec b_{3d}\times\vec b_{1d}} {\|\vec b_{3d}\times\vec b_{1d}\|} \rightarrow R_d=[\vec b_{2d}\times\vec b_{3d},\vec b_{2d},\vec b_{3d}]\)

\(M=-k_Re_R-k_\Omega e_\Omega+\Omega{\times}J\Omega\)
\(\quad -J(\hat{\Omega}R^TR_d\Omega_d-R^TR_d\dot{\Omega}_d)\)
Geometric Control · Stability

Proposition 1 — Exponential Stability of Attitude

Statement
Suppose \(\Psi(R(0),R_d(0)) < 2\) and $$\|e_\Omega(0)\|^2 < \frac{2k_R}{\lambda_{\max}(J)}\bigl(2 - \Psi(R(0),R_d(0))\bigr).$$ Then \((e_R, e_\Omega) = (0,0)\) is exponentially stable.
Proof sketch — Lyapunov analysis:
\(V_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}e_\Omega^T J e_\Omega + k_R\,\Psi(R, R_d)\)
\(z_2 = \begin{bmatrix}\|e_R\| \\ \|e_\Omega\|\end{bmatrix}\)
\(z_2^T M_{21}\, z_2 \leq V_1 \leq z_2^T M_{22}\, z_2\)
\(\dot{V}_1 \leq -z_2^T W_2\, z_2 \implies \dot{V}_1 \leq -\beta_1 V_1\)
Geometric Control · Stability

Proposition 2 — Exponential Stability of Full System

Statement
Suppose \(\Psi(R(0),R_d(0)) < 1\) and the controller gains satisfy certain growth conditions. Then \((e_x, e_v, e_R, e_\Omega) = 0\) is exponentially stable.
Position tracking is driven by attitude error through thrust direction mismatch: \(Re_3 \neq R_d e_3\) when \(e_R \neq 0\).
Proof sketch — Lyapunov analysis:
\(V = \tfrac{k_x}{2}\|e_x\|^2 + \tfrac{m}{2}\|e_v\|^2 + c\,e_x^T e_v + V_1\)
\(z_1 = \begin{bmatrix}\|e_x\| \\ \|e_v\|\end{bmatrix},\quad z_2 = \begin{bmatrix}\|e_R\| \\ \|e_\Omega\|\end{bmatrix}\)
\(z_1^T M_{11} z_1 + z_2^T M_{21} z_2 \leq V \leq z_1^T M_{12} z_1 + z_2^T M_{22}' z_2\)
\(\dot{V} \leq -z_1^T \tilde{W}_1 z_1 + z_1^T W_{12} z_2 - z_2^T W_2 z_2 = -z^T \tilde{W} z \implies \dot{V} \leq -\beta V\)
Geometric Control · Stability

Proposition 3 — Almost-Global Exponential Attractiveness

Definition — Exponential Attractiveness
\(z=0\) is exponentially attractive if \(\exists\,\delta>0\) and \(\alpha(\delta),\beta>0\) such that \(\|z(0)\|<\delta \implies \|z(t)\|\le\alpha(\delta)e^{-\beta t}\). Weaker than stability: \(\alpha\) need not be proportional to \(\|z(0)\|\).
Statement
Suppose \(1 \leq \Psi(R(0),R_d(0)) < 2\) and \(\|e_\Omega(0)\|^2 < \tfrac{2k_R}{\lambda_{\max}(J)}(2-\Psi(R(0),R_d(0)))\). Then the zero equilibrium is almost-globally exponentially attractive.
"Almost-global": the unstable set is a codimension-1 manifold of measure zero — a topological obstruction intrinsic to SO(3).
Proof sketch:
  • Initial conditions satisfy Prop. 1 → \((e_R, e_\Omega) \to 0\) exponentially
  • \(\exists\, t^*\): rotational state enters region of attraction of Prop. 2
  • On \([0,t^*]\): define \(V_3 = \tfrac{1}{2}\|e_x\|^2 + \tfrac{m}{2}\|e_v\|^2\). Gronwall's inequality gives \(\dot V_3 \leq (d_1+d_2)V_3\) → \(z_1\) bounded on \([0,t^*]\)
  • For \(t \geq t^*\): Prop. 2 applies → \(\|z(t)\| \leq C e^{-\beta(t-t^*)}\)
  • Combine: \(\|z(t)\| \leq \alpha(\delta)\,e^{-\beta t}\) for all \(t > 0\)
Geometric Control · Implementation

Implementation Challenges

Isaac Sim / Pegasus convention: \(z\)-axis points upward
Lee et al. use a downward inertial \(e_3\). In implementation, \(e_3\) is upward, so gravity and the desired thrust-axis direction flip signs.
Implemented dynamics:
\(m\ddot{x} = -mg e_3 + f R e_3\)
Implemented trajectory-tracking force:
\(A = -k_x e_x - k_v e_v + mg e_3 + m\ddot{x}_d\)

\(\vec{b}_{3d} = \dfrac{A}{\|A\|}, \quad f = A \cdot Re_3\)
The sign flip affects gravity compensation and the direction of \(\vec b_{3d}\), but the closed-loop error structure remains the same when all signs are changed consistently.
Analytic feed-forward terms
$$\Omega_d = \begin{bmatrix} b_{3d}^\top\dot{b}_{2d} \\ b_{1d}^\top\dot{b}_{3d} \\ b_{2d}^\top\dot{b}_{1d} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \dot\Omega_d = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{b}_{3d}^\top\dot{b}_{2d} + b_{3d}^\top\ddot{b}_{2d} \\ \dot{b}_{1d}^\top\dot{b}_{3d} + b_{1d}^\top\ddot{b}_{3d} \\ \dot{b}_{2d}^\top\dot{b}_{1d} + b_{2d}^\top\ddot{b}_{1d} \end{bmatrix}$$
Control allocation matrix \(\Lambda\)
$$\begin{bmatrix} f \\ M_1 \\ M_2 \\ M_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} k_f & k_f & k_f & k_f \\ -\tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & \tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & \tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & -\tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} \\ -\tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & \tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & -\tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} & \tfrac{k_f d}{\sqrt{2}} \\ -k_m & -k_m & k_m & k_m \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u_0 \\ u_1 \\ u_2 \\ u_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Conclusion

Conclusions & Future Work

Findings
  • Isaac Sim PhysX: 13–37 cm RMS position error on the elliptic-helix trajectory
  • Custom RK4 solver: ~0.9 cm RMS — 15× improvement with identical parameters
  • Dominant error source: PhysX integration, not vehicle model fidelity
  • Articulation model (E2/E3) is more accurate than single-rigid-body baseline (E1)
  • Forward Euler at 250 Hz is competitive with RK4 at the same rate
Future Work
  • Measure rolling-moment coefficient \(k_m\) on a torque bench
  • Hardware experiments to close the sim-to-real loop
  • Investigate tighter PhysX solver settings (sub-step count, contact solver iterations)
  • Extend benchmark to agile trajectories and hover perturbation experiments
